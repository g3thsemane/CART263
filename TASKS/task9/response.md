Final Project Response

1. Lucy & Jake, https://luciee246.github.io/cart263/projects/project-II/

I found this project done by Lucy and Jake to be quite nice. The concept of the piece is fairly simple, but that doesn't take away from its fun game loop. An element I found to be done very well was the tactility(?) and responsiveness of the UI. When answering a question correctly, the combination of visual cues and sound effects to imply success are very satisfactory. When making a game, there's no doubt that fun and playability are of much importance, and I find this project to have achieved a good sense of fun. In my personal experience, I was able to play this game for a good while before moving on. Another aspect of the project I found to be cool is the multiplayer and memory capabilities. From what I can tell, there is use of a database in order to store chat history. If I am not mistaken, a database would require a backend, something which I have no experience with.

2. Philippe & Ben, https://philippe-bn.github.io/cart263/projects/project-2/

Done by Philippe and Ben, "Escapism" is a project that caught my eye due to its fluid, colorful environment. I find the scene created in this project to be quite beautiful. The combination of tall green hills, coupled with the fog and flying flamingos, makes you wonder what world you are witnessing exists. I really enjoy placing the camera in a certain area, and just watching the scene live over time. The combination of the sun and the fog applies an early day atmosphere to the environment. While sitting in one specific spot, it's nice to observe the flying flamingos and their flight patterns. I wasn't able to find it in the code, but I wonder how the flight path of the birds is set up. I assume there is some sort of sine wave situation happening there.

3. Alex, Kai, Emmett, https://crooksnwhiskey.github.io/3Dproject/

This puzzle game made by Alex, Kai, and Emmett is a fun interactive piece which critiques surveillance. I found the simple and constrained scene layout did a good job at playing up the idea of surveillance and its restrictive effects. It took me a second to even figure out what I was supposed to do, but eventually I got into the concept of the puzzle and found it to be quite fun. Unfortunately, I reached a point where I just had to give up cause I didn't want to lose my mind. The game is accompanied by a minimal soundtrack that causes a sense of urgency. Due to my "rage quitting" I will not see the end of the puzzle, but I do wonder what it may contain.
